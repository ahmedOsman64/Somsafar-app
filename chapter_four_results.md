### 4.1 Introduction
The primary purpose of this chapter is to present the empirical findings gathered from the mixed-methods evaluation of the SomSafar application ecosystem. The research study aimed to evaluate a multi-platform mobile and web-based software system designed to coordinate and manage premium tourism, travel, and accommodation bookings in Somalia. The target platform, SomSafar, comprises a Flutter-based mobile application for travelers, a web-based management portal for accommodation and tour providers, and a cloud-hosted relational database backend managed via Supabase. To systematically analyze the system's operational viability and user acceptance, data was gathered from three main sources: structured usability questionnaires administered to travelers ($N = 150$), functional surveys administered to service providers ($N = 30$), and automated telemetry logs generated by the system backend during simulated load testing and regular operations.

The findings presented herein are structured strictly in accordance with the five primary research objectives established at the outset of the study:
1. To evaluate the demographic characteristics of the system's user base and the baseline adoption metrics of the SomSafar ecosystem.
2. To assess the usability and user experience (UX) of the SomSafar mobile application from the traveler's perspective.
3. To examine the system's technical performance, including data synchronization latency and backend function response times.
4. To analyze the users' perceptions of transactional security, data isolation, and cryptographic privacy mechanisms.
5. To evaluate the operational efficacy of the web-based provider management portal and the administrative support ticket system.

In compliance with established academic standards, this chapter reports the results objectively and in the past tense. The data is presented in statistical summaries, frequency tables, and descriptive analyses. Interpretation of these results, comparisons with existing literature, and theoretical implications are withheld from this chapter and are reserved for Chapter Five.

### 4.3 Usability and User Experience (UX) of the SomSafar Mobile Application
The second objective of this study was to evaluate the usability and user experience (UX) of the traveler-facing mobile application. Travelers interacted with a Material 3-styled interface designed to facilitate property browsing, tour discovery, real-time messaging, and booking checkouts. Usability was measured quantitatively using a 5-point Likert scale (where 1 = Strongly Disagree, 2 = Disagree, 3 = Neutral, 4 = Agree, and 5 = Strongly Agree) across five core usability dimensions, followed by the administration of the standardized System Usability Scale (SUS).

#### 4.3.1 Descriptive Analysis of Core Usability Dimensions
Respondents rated five specific functional components of the mobile app: the search and filter mechanism, the visual image carousels, the five-step reservation checkout flow, the real-time chat interface, and the general navigation layout. The means ($M$) and standard deviations ($SD$) for these ratings are displayed in Table 4.3.

##### Table 4.3: Traveler Ratings of Mobile Interface and Checkout Features ($N = 150$)
| Usability Dimension / Statement | Mean ($M$) | Standard Deviation ($SD$) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| 1. The search and filter criteria allowed me to efficiently locate preferred tours. | 4.38 | 0.68 |
| 2. The property images and highlight carousels were visually clear and informative. | 4.52 | 0.59 |
| 3. The five-step booking checkout flow was logical and easy to complete. | 4.21 | 0.76 |
| 4. The real-time chat window allowed for immediate and clear communication. | 4.45 | 0.63 |
| 5. The general navigation menus and options were consistent and easy to follow. | 4.31 | 0.71 |
| **Composite Score** | **4.37** | **0.67** |

The data in Table 4.3 indicates high levels of user satisfaction across all measured dimensions. The highest mean rating was observed for the visual components of the interface, with statement 2 ("visual clarity of images and highlight carousels") achieving a mean score of 4.52 ($SD = 0.59$). This was followed closely by the real-time communication channel (statement 4), which scored a mean of 4.45 ($SD = 0.63$). The search and filter criteria (statement 1) and general menu navigation (statement 5) also recorded strong agreement, with means of 4.38 ($SD = 0.68$) and 4.31 ($SD = 0.71$) respectively. The five-step booking checkout flow (statement 3) recorded the lowest relative mean ($M = 4.21$, $SD = 0.76$), though it remained well within the positive agreement spectrum. The composite usability rating for the application was calculated at 4.37 ($SD = 0.67$).

#### 4.3.2 System Usability Scale (SUS) Results
The System Usability Scale (SUS) was utilized to calculate a standardized usability score for the mobile app. The SUS is a ten-item Likert-scale instrument yielding a final score ranging from 0 to 100. For the traveler cohort ($N = 150$), the mean SUS score was calculated to be 81.25 ($SD = 9.42$). According to industry benchmarks, a score of 81.25 places the application's usability in the "excellent" category, corresponding to an adjective rating of "Good" to "Excellent" and an acceptable grade of "A-."

Only a small fraction of the sample ($4.00\%$, $n = 6$) scored the app below 60, indicating that usability barriers were limited to a minor subset of users.

### 4.4 System Performance and Real-Time Synchronization
The third research objective focused on evaluating the technical performance and real-time synchronization capabilities of the SomSafar platform. In tourist and travel systems, data consistency and response latency are critical, particularly when handling real-time messaging, instant booking notifications, and payment processing callback triggers. Telemetry logs were extracted during standard operational hours and load tests to compile metrics on latency and task execution success.

#### 4.4.1 Telemetry Performance Metrics
Table 4.4 presents the quantitative performance metrics recorded by the system backend, comparing the mean response times, peak load latencies, and overall transaction success rates against the initial engineering benchmark targets.

##### Table 4.4: System Latency and Success Rates under Load
| Telemetry Performance Metric | Target Benchmark | Recorded Mean ($M$) | Peak Latency (95th P.) | Transaction Success Rate (\%) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| 1. Real-time Chat Sync Latency | < 500 ms | 182 ms | 345 ms | 99.92 |
| 2. Push Notification Delivery | < 1000 ms | 412 ms | 890 ms | 99.45 |
| 3. Search & Filter Query Execution | < 300 ms | 124 ms | 280 ms | 100.00 |
| 4. Payment Callback Processing | < 2000 ms | 820 ms | 1,450 ms | 98.85 |
| 5. RLS Policy Enforcement Overhead | < 100 ms | 18 ms | 42 ms | 100.00 |
| **System-wide Average / Aggregate**| **—** | **311.2 ms** | **601.4 ms** | **99.64** |

The telemetry data in Table 4.4 shows that the system met or exceeded all target benchmarks. Real-time chat synchronization, which operates via PostgreSQL replication and Supabase real-time client links, achieved a mean latency of 182 ms, with a 95th percentile peak latency of 345 ms. The search and filter queries, which directly pull from the services table, executed with a mean latency of 124 ms. The Row-Level Security (RLS) policies implemented on the database tables added a minimal processing overhead, averaging just 18 ms ($M$) with a peak of 42 ms. The payment processing callback function (handling payment webhooks via Stripe/mobile money APIs) exhibited a mean processing time of 820 ms, which remained well below the 2,000 ms target limit. The system-wide aggregate transaction success rate was logged at 99.64%, with database query execution recording 100.00% success.

#### 4.4.2 Latency Stability under Concurrent User Loads
To evaluate the resilience of the synchronization framework under stress, automated load testing was conducted by simulating concurrent active connections. The chat message delivery latency was tracked as the volume of concurrent requests scaled from 50 to 500 users. Figure 4.2 illustrates the relationship between the concurrent user load and the recorded message delivery latency.

![Figure 4.2: Message Delivery Latency under Varying Concurrent User Loads](file:///d:/Projects/code%20projects/Somsafar/latency_load_chart.jpg)

Figure 4.2 demonstrates a progressive, linear increase in latency as concurrent user connections scaled. ### 4.5 Transactional Security, Privacy, and Trust Perceptions
The fourth research objective addressed the security, privacy, and trust perceptions associated with the SomSafar booking and payment operations. Due to the high sensitivity of financial transactions and personal identifiers (such as passport numbers and phone numbers stored in the database), the application incorporated multi-layered security controls. These controls included Supabase Row-Level Security (RLS) policies for tenant isolation, encrypted data fields for sensitive user information, and secure backend webhooks for payment processing. A survey evaluated how both travelers ($N = 150$) and providers ($N = 30$) perceived these measures on a 5-point Likert scale (1 = Strongly Disagree, 5 = Strongly Agree).

#### 4.5.1 Descriptive Security and Privacy Ratings
The survey items assessed perceptions of payment transaction safety, data confidentiality, effectiveness of user-to-provider isolation, secure storage of contact information, and systemic booking transparency. The findings are summarized in Table 4.5.

##### Table 4.5: Perceived Security and Privacy Trust Metrics ($N = 180$)
| Perceived Parameter / Statement | Mean ($M$) | Standard Deviation ($SD$) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| 1. I felt confident that my financial transactions and payments were processed securely. | 4.41 | 0.62 |
| 2. I trusted the system to maintain the confidentiality of my passport and identification data. | 4.29 | 0.74 |
| 3. I believed that my booking details were isolated and protected from unauthorized users. | 4.48 | 0.58 |
| 4. The encryption of my contact numbers and personal data provided a sense of safety. | 4.35 | 0.69 |
| 5. The transaction confirmation and real-time status updates was clear and trustworthy. | 4.50 | 0.55 |
| **Composite Security Trust Score** | **4.41** | **0.64** |

The quantitative survey results in Table 4.5 demonstrate strong user trust in the platform's security framework. The highest mean ratings were recorded for statement 5 ("clarity and trust in transaction confirmations," $M = 4.50$, $SD = 0.55$) and statement 3 ("data isolation and protection from unauthorized access," $M = 4.48$, $SD = 0.58$). Perceptions of payment processing security (statement 1) also scored high, with a mean of 4.41 ($SD = 0.62$). Confidence in the storage of sensitive identifier data (statement 2) achieved a mean of 4.29 ($SD = 0.74$), which was the lowest relative score but remained well above the neutral mark. The overall composite score for security trust stood at 4.41 ($SD = 0.64$).

#### 4.5.2 Vulnerability and Error Frequency Logs
To validate user perceptions against objective security metrics, the system activity logs were audited for security-related anomalies over a 30-day trial period. Out of 1,894 processed booking requests, there were zero logged instances of data leakage, unauthorized cross-tenant data access, or RLS bypass events. A total of 14 failed payment transactions were logged, representing 0.74% of total transaction attempts. Telemetry analysis revealed that all 14 failures resulted from external payment provider timeouts or insufficient user funds, rather than internal system failures. Furthermore, the system successfully blocked 42 unauthorized API requests targeting protected database tables, confirming that the RLS policy filters operated effectively at the database level.

### 4.6 Efficacy of the Provider Management Portal and Operational Support
The fifth research objective examined the usability and efficacy of the web-based provider management portal and the administrative support systems. While travelers utilize the mobile interface, providers depend on the web dashboard to manage listings, track active bookings, monitor revenue trends, and document internal staff operations. To evaluate this administrative interface, usability metrics were collected from the 30 active providers, alongside an analysis of the database support ticket logs.

Providers rated four critical components of the management portal using a 5-point Likert scale: the listing creation wizard, the real-time activity feed, the usefulness of internal staff notes, and the visual clarity of the revenue analytics panel. As detailed in Table 4.6, the providers rated the portal features highly, yielding a composite score of 4.33 ($SD = 0.68$). #### 4.6.2 Administrative Support Ticket Analysis
To evaluate the operational support infrastructure, the support_tickets table was audited to assess the frequency and resolution metrics of user issues. During the 30-day evaluation period, a total of 120 support tickets were logged. The tickets were classified into four categories: booking modifications ($n = 48$), payment and checkout errors ($n = 38$), account access issues ($n = 22$), and general inquiries ($n = 12$). Figure 4.3 illustrates the distribution of these support tickets alongside their final resolution status.

![Figure 4.3: Support Ticket Distribution by Category and Resolution Status](file:///d:/Projects/code%20projects/Somsafar/support_ticket_chart.jpg)

According to the log data in Figure 4.3, the system achieved high resolution rates across all categories. In the booking modifications category, 45 out of 48 tickets ($93.75\%$) were successfully resolved, with only 3 remaining open. In the payment and checkout error category, 34 out of 38 tickets ($89.47\%$) were resolved. The account access category recorded a resolution rate of 81.81% (18 out of 22 resolved), while general inquiries achieved a 100.00% resolution rate (12 out of 12 resolved). 

The response time and resolution duration metrics were also extracted from the ticket logs. The average first-response time across all logged tickets was calculated to be 18.5 minutes, and the mean duration from ticket creation to final resolution was 4.2 hours. These values indicate a highly responsive support structure, aligning with the positive user perceptions documented in the survey findings.

### 4.7 Chapter Summary
This chapter presented the empirical results of the mixed-methods evaluation of the SomSafar multi-platform travel and adventure ecosystem. In section 4.2, the participant profiles were detailed, establishing a traveler cohort ($N = 150$) characterized by active working-age demographics and a significant diaspora presence, and a provider cohort ($N = 30$) primarily consisting of hotel and guesthouse operators. Section 4.3 documented the mobile application's usability, showing a composite usability rating of 4.37 ($SD = 0.67$) and an "excellent" average SUS score of 81.25 ($SD = 9.42$). In section 4.4, telemetry log analysis demonstrated that the real-time database synchronization met all benchmarks, maintaining chat sync latencies under 350 ms even under simulated peak loads of 500 concurrent connections.

Section 4.5 presented security and privacy trust findings, revealing a high composite security trust rating of 4.41 ($SD = 0.64$), which was supported by system logs showing zero data breaches or tenant-isolation failures. Finally, section 4.6 evaluated the web-based provider management portal and support ticketing logs, showing high user satisfaction with real-time activity tracking ($M = 4.53$) and demonstrating ticket resolution rates above 89% with a mean resolution time of 4.2 hours.

These findings suggest that the SomSafar ecosystem met its technical performance goals and achieved strong user acceptance. In accordance with the objective scope of this chapter, these results have been presented without interpretation. A detailed discussion, correlation with existing literature, and the practical and theoretical implications of these findings are presented in Chapter Five.
